    <h2 id="process-section-2-heading">
        2. Process Explained Like You're Five
    </h2>

    <h3>Imagine a Recipe Book</h3>

    <p>
        Think about a recipe book on a shelf.
        The book contains instructions for making food.
        That book is like a <strong>program</strong>.
    </p>

    <p>
        When someone opens the book and starts cooking,
        the cooking activity is like a <strong>process</strong>.
    </p>

    <p>
        The recipe itself is not cooking.
        The process is the real work happening in the kitchen.
    </p>

    <h3>What Does the Kitchen Need?</h3>

    <p>While cooking, the cook needs:</p>

    <ul>
        <li>Space on the counter (like memory)</li>
        <li>Time at the stove (like CPU time)</li>
        <li>Pots, pans, and ingredients (like resources)</li>
        <li>A way to know what step comes next (like the program counter)</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
        The operating system is like a kitchen manager.
        It gives each cooking activity (process) the space and time it needs,
        and makes sure different cooks do not spoil each other's work.
    </p>

    <h3>Key Idea for Young Learners</h3>

    <p>
        A program is the set of instructions.
        A process is those instructions actually being followed.
    </p>
